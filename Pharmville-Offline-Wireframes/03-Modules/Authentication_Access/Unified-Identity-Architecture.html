Unified identity, authentication and workspace architecture

Proposed / Pending Approval / Not Implemented. This rework defines one logical Authentication module using the existing identity database. D-003, D-023 and D-024 remain open; D-028 records this unified design and its proposed schema/API extension. No existing role or permission record is approved or seeded here.

One person, one identity

identity.users.id is the common subject for Customer, Rider, Pharmacist and every staff identity. identity.user_profiles stores shared profile data; customer.profiles, practitioner and rider records extend that subject without passwords or separate login databases. A verified identifier resolves an existing subject; adding a role or workspace never creates another user. Email and phone unique constraints prevent duplicate identifiers, not proof that two unknown identifiers belong to different people. Linking/recovering identities needs proof of both contacts or the reviewed assisted process; never merge by display name, invitation, or an unverified matching email.

Authentication owns proofs, factors, sessions and access-policy evaluation. Customer Accounts owns customer onboarding/profile/consent. Admin is a management surface invoking Identity commands; it cannot directly grant arbitrary SQL or impersonate a subject. Workspace navigation consumes a permission-filtered projection and grants no access itself.

flowchart TD
  Entry["Common identifier entry"] --> Identity["One identity and challenge policy"]
  Identity --> Proof["OTP or password; required authenticator MFA"]
  Proof --> Session["One session and verified method timestamps"]
  Session --> Grants["Active assignments and scoped permissions"]
  Grants --> Contexts["Authorized workspace choices"]
  Contexts --> StepUp["Step-up when target policy is stronger"]
  StepUp --> Context["Backend-selected active context"]
  Context --> API["Independent API and resource authorization"]

Login and enumeration resistance

  1. The common entry accepts a phone or email identifier, never a role/panel. Normalize using a reviewed identifier policy. Phone uses OTP; email starts password proof. This public presentation is determined by identifier syntax, never account existence. The backend resolves actual account policy privately.
  2. Return the same 202 challenge shape and expiry budget for known, unknown, locked and suspended identifiers. Use opaque random tokens, keyed hashes at rest, equivalent verification work for unknown credentials and throttling that does not reveal the account. No role, enrollment state, delivery receipt or masked stored contact appears before valid proof. Phone OTP dispatch follows existing WhatsApp-primary/SMS-fallback policy; email OTP is not invented.
  3. Validate/consume the durable challenge atomically. Unknown-phone customer onboarding may create one identity only after OTP verification and uniqueness recheck. Existing first-name/consent requirements still govern customer profile creation. Email proof never silently provisions staff.
  4. Privileged Super Admin, Central Admin and Finance Manager password login completes authenticator MFA before a privileged-capable session. Customer/rider OTP may establish a low-assurance session for those permitted surfaces even when the same person has privileged roles; it never unlocks their staff workspace.
  5. Return the authenticated subject and session plus permission-filtered workspace choices. No assignment yields a restricted account-access state with only identity/workspace/step-up/logout operations. It does not manufacture a Customer or Staff grant.
  6. Activate a context only after backend permission, scope, assignment validity and required-method freshness checks. Each protected operation repeats them; a valid signature or visible menu is insufficient.

Workspace and scope model

User-Roles.md is the canonical role-to-workspace policy table. Business role codes remain proposed masters; workspace codes and navigation permission codes are versioned application policy, not additional database tables or seed rows.

Each offered context binds session + identity + one assignment + workspace + resolved organization/location/warehouse + assignment version + policy version + expiry in a server-signed opaque contextToken. The token is only a selector. The backend reloads the assignment and scope on selection and every protected request. Do not union unrelated assignments or combine one site's grant with another site's resource. A global grant remains explicitly global, not a fallback for missing scope.

Existing SQL represents global grants as (scope_type, scope_id) = (NULL, NULL); preserve that convention. Organization and location scopes reference their respective UUIDs through validated service joins. Existing warehouse scope references warehouse.warehouses.id, then resolves its organization/location. A store is an active organization.locations row of the permitted store type. Unknown/dangling scope values grant nothing. The polymorphic fields are not physical FKs: the writer must validate the target and hierarchy inside its transaction. Membership alone is not a permission grant; its organization/status/location and the resource still constrain staff access. A single membership row is not proof of access to every location.

A workspace entry permission (for example workspace.pharmacist.enter) and each business action permission are both required. Customer data additionally uses subject ownership; pharmacist access needs existing credential/site/patient/evidence gates; Rider access needs current task/shift; Finance cannot read prescriptions. A Super Admin menu does not bypass clinical or finance invariants.

Default, switching and step-up

The default is the current still-authorized context; on a new session, exactly one ready context may be offered as a default hint, otherwise return null and show a chooser. A hint is never activation. Client last-workspace preferences may order choices but cannot grant access. No cross-role default priority is invented.

Context switching takes an opaque selector and session-context ETag. Revalidate live grants/scope and the target's required verified methods. A stronger target returns AUTH_STEP_UP_REQUIRED; prove missing password/authenticator methods in a session- and target-bound challenge chain, then retry context activation. Store independent password/OTP/MFA timestamps; verifying one method never refreshes another method's age. Account lock/suspension blocks step-up as well as login.

The active assignment/workspace and monotonic context_version are stored on the session. Each switch returns a new access token with that generation. Older generations fail AUTH_CONTEXT_STALE, including other tabs. Scope/organization changes are context changes, not arbitrary request headers. Refresh follows the current valid context and cannot resurrect a revoked assignment. Lost switch responses are recovered by reading current identity/context; no secret response is cached.

Authorization consistency and revocation

Proposed default: primary-database evaluation, no positive authorization cache. Check current user status, session revocation/expiry/generation, selected assignment activity/time window, role/permission deletion, policy freshness, membership and resource scope. Database or policy unavailability fails closed (503), never stale JWT permission fallback. JWT role claims are non-authoritative hints.

A request's authorization linearization point is its locked authoritative check. Business writes and protected file/export grants hold the required user/session/assignment/role/permission/scope locks through commit. Identity suspension/assignment writers serialize on the same subject; role-permission writers lock the role before changing edges. Use a consistent ordered lock strategy and bounded deadlock retry before any external effect. A request authorized first may finish before a waiting revocation commits; all authorizations after the revocation commit deny the removed access. Already returned bytes cannot be recalled. In-flight streams/long jobs recheck at documented bounded checkpoints, and signed downloads use short-lived grants with an authorization check at issuance/redemption. D-003 must approve freshness budgets and any future bounded-cache/stream exceptions; no undocumented TTL window is allowed.

Removing one assignment invalidates its active context immediately under that policy; other valid assignments remain available without a new account. Suspending/closing an identity or changing its password revokes all sessions and pending proofs. Normal logout revokes the requested owned session and refresh lineage; staff administrative revocation uses audited Identity commands. Every API rechecks account/session state despite unexpired JWTs.

Durable proof and credential lifecycle

Persistence assessment separates existing structures, added proposed constraints and service-enforced invariants. Durable authentication_challenges records replace signed-only MFA state. Lock/consume a challenge once; its binding and parent proof, user/factor/session versions, policy, attempt budget and expiry must match. Expired or failed proofs never create a session. Parent consumption and successor creation are one transaction; failure increments attempts/audit in a separate committed failure outcome rather than rolling those counters back with a successful-auth transaction.

TOTP verification locks the factor and atomically advances last_accepted_totp_counter; the same counter cannot satisfy another challenge, including enrollment/step-up/recovery. Never log the code/secret. Each refresh token has retained hashed lineage; one active token per session is mirrored by sessions.refresh_token_hash. Consume old token and insert its unique child atomically. Recognized spent-token replay revokes the entire family. Clients serialize refresh and reauthenticate after a lost successful response; do not replay refresh blindly or cache raw credentials in idempotency records.

Password changes verify current password and required recent MFA, enforce approved history/policy, store only hashes, revoke sessions/proofs and require reauthentication. Self-service forgotten-password recovery requires verified-phone OTP and an already verified authenticator; accounts lacking these use a reviewed assisted process. Authenticator loss/reset is assisted only pending D-003; OTP alone cannot remove MFA or activate a privileged assignment. First authenticator enrollment requires recent verified-phone OTP and password with current staff eligibility; replacement requires current MFA too. Exact enrollment/bootstrap/recovery and policy budgets remain proposed for Security approval, not an invented implemented procedure.

Transport and audit

Bearer transport is the fully specified proposal. Store access credentials in client memory; refresh credentials use platform secure storage. Browser cookie transport is a separate D-003 choice requiring Secure/HttpOnly cookies, reviewed SameSite/domain policy, Origin checks and CSRF tokens on mutations including login/logout/context; do not silently switch the bearer contracts to cookie behavior. Rotate keys and validate issuer/audience/type; token lifetimes, clock skew, password hashing parameters and rate budgets are owner-approved configuration, never schema defaults promoted to security policy.

Audit login/denial, method changes, context selection, assignment/permission edits, revocation, refresh replay and recovery with safe actor/target references and request/correlation IDs. Never record passwords, OTPs, authenticator secrets, refresh/access/challenge/context tokens or encrypted identifier bodies in logs/audit payloads. Notifications/outbox occur after commit and cannot repeat source credential or grant changes.

Compatibility and scope

API compatibility preserves API-001/002, API-089–093 and existing staff administration IDs. Proposed unified routes use new IDs and the same Identity command model. Legacy routes are adapters, not another authentication subsystem; none is removed or declared deprecated at runtime. D-023 replacement-contact proof remains a distinct unresolved flow and is not solved by login recovery. P/M/O, clinical review, mandatory delivery OTP/photo and finance invariants remain unchanged.

Single-session and onboarding constraints

AUTH-02's one-session-per-staff-account rule is identity-wide across staff workspaces, not one session per panel. Lock the subject when issuing or first upgrading a staff-capable session; revoke the previous staff-capable session/lineage before commit. Context switching inside that session never creates an extra account or session. Rider/customer session limits retain their existing policy and require D-003's exact configured budgets.

A verified-phone onboarding flow may request a Customer assignment only through Customer Accounts after required profile/consent gates and the approved default-customer provisioning policy. Login itself does not grant roles. No completed onboarding or eligible assignment means restricted account access. During step-up, record each actually verified method timestamp independently even if another required method is still pending; no privileged access follows until all target requirements hold. First-enrollment eligibility is not MFA bypass.

Account-control boundary and lost-response recovery

Owned-session controls (identity, workspace discovery, context selection/step-up, own credential/factor management and logout) do not require an already active business assignment. They require live account/session checks and their own proof/target eligibility; auth.self never authorizes an order, clinical record or admin grant. API-100 alone accepts a stale context generation to read current session state. After a lost context-switch response, read API-100 and rotate the still-current refresh credential through API-091 to obtain an access token for the current generation; if that refresh outcome is uncertain, reauthenticate. No stale token becomes valid for business APIs through this recovery path.

Routine non-privileged staff OTP was an inherited proposal. D-028 now proposes password plus authenticator as the staff default in the role matrix; this change requires D-003 approval. Customer/Rider OTP and required AUTH-01 privileged password/authenticator are preserved. No approved policy is silently replaced.

Failure counters and challenge versions

Read status, locked_until, account_closed_at and failed_login_count from the locked identity when evaluating proof eligibility; a valid JWT does not override temporary lockout. Password/step-up failures commit the shared account counter/lock state as well as challenge attempts and redacted login-attempt evidence. When that same transaction changes users.row_version solely for its own failed-attempt counter, update that owned challenge's identity_version to the resulting version; preserve its attempt budget and binding. A lock blocks the challenge. An independent password, status, contact or grant/security-policy change never receives this exception: invalidate the affected proof and require a fresh flow. Do not reset global/per-identifier/IP throttles by starting another challenge. Scope-related table joins in per-operation maps are conditional on the selected/target scope; global NULL/NULL does not imply a warehouse lookup or a grant.

Existing onboarding dependency remains open

JAPI-15 / API-004 still needs the approved first-profile creation branch, actual required firstName and consent handling (D-003/D-023/D-024). A completed phone proof can create the central identity and a restricted session without a customer profile or grant; that success does not complete the customer onboarding journey. The future owned profile-completion branch must work from account-access state, then invoke the narrow default-Customer provisioning command after approved gates. Until that contract is approved, new-customer commerce entry remains blocked safely. Do not invent blank first_name data or an automatic role from the login identifier.

Workspace permission projection

Effective permissions are the intersection of the selected assignment's active role permissions and the workspace's approved capability policy, followed by organization/site/resource and method-freshness checks. An entry permission alone grants no action; an action permission alone cannot enter an unrelated panel. Each business operation enforces its applicable workspace entry policy as well as its own action/resource policy. Shared operations may explicitly permit multiple workspace contexts, with independent scoped checks. Never infer this allowlist from frontend routes or client role strings; the role matrix restrictions and existing per-operation consumer/permission contracts are its review inputs under D-003/D-024. API-093 projects only the current intersection, never every permission of every role. Account-control and workload/provider trust exceptions remain as explicitly documented above.

Scoped offboarding versus identity suspension

Changing identity-wide status additionally requires identity.accounts.manage in explicit global scope with fresh password/authenticator; organization/store staff-management permission alone cannot suspend or reactivate a shared person. Use API-110 for scoped assignment removal; it must preserve Customer and other organization access. Suspension and allowed reviewed reactivation remain separate from lock/recovery bypass. This is a proposed D-028 authorization clarification for the retained API-034 status field, not an approved permission grant. The global status authority/delegation process requires D-003/D-024 approval.
